# Find what the refinement settles

Once the gaps are resolved and the decisions in play carried, find what the refinement settled
that binds after the task's merge, through `find-foundation-entries` with the task as its caller:
the source is the task document, the conversation that refined it and the material the task
started from; the next reader is the task's cycle; the mode is rows of the document's section
*Settlements to record* (WAY-134). This runs for every task, whatever its project relation: a
project member and a source-only task add their own settlements beside the projected entries they
carry.

Refinement searches no more of the foundation than step 1 reads and *Decisions in play* carries.
The one search of the whole foundation is the cycle's, when it writes each kept row (WAY-33); a
kept row that search finds already stated becomes no second entry, and one whose meaning cannot
hold against what it finds goes back to the human, as WAY-134 gives both.

Where the document already holds accepted rows from an earlier refinement, a row the human
reopens, or whose source this refinement changed or removed, is a candidate again; every other
accepted row enters the working table `accepted` and is not cut again. The counts of the
`Candidates:` line cover this refinement's working table.

A row that repeats a choice the human made between ways shown, such as an answer under *Questions
to settle* that keeps its *Not chosen* or a settled technology question of a run of
`settle-technology`, enters the table already `accepted`, marked as the human's choice, and stays
unless the human strikes it; the human accepts or rejects only the rows you derived from the
material or from the human's loose words (WAY-115, WAY-134); this holds only until the task's cycle
starts (WAY-315). The run writes no row, and the cycle writes no decision of a technology choice
otherwise, so its settled question is always shown.

A candidate that collides with a projected entry or with a current decision the human has not
agreed to change goes back to step 4 as a conflict; one that changes the task's scope goes back to
step 3. The candidate of a question whose chosen option changes a decision (WAY-314) is no conflict
with that decision, and its row names the decision it changes.
