# Findings of the review of the whole change — the findings the fix is given

Source: `reports/review-whole-report.md` (FAIL, reviewed head b64608400f4fb55bb5508f71e2c4c2aadbcd6a60). The four Important findings block; the three Minor findings are within the task and are fixed in the same round (WAY-161). Words are the reviewer's.

## Important

### I-1. The committed record copy never holds the CI final gate, so the next cycle's step 4 loses its list of failing tests and runs a full local gate on its base every time

- Place: `task-cycle/skill/SKILL.md:159-171` (step 4) against `task-cycle/skill/SKILL.md:613-621` (step 12) and `task-cycle/record_template.md:149-159`; commit `b646084`.
- What is wrong: step 12 writes the CI run's result into *Verification* of the **original** record only after the record's pair of commits is pushed, and says: "The committed copy of the record holds that row only for the runs before its own head: the run on the head that removes the copy comes after it, and no pair of commits is repeated to bring that row in." When CI is the final gate, the cycle also runs no local gate at step 10. So the copy `docs/task-cycles/<slug>/cycle.md` that the branch adds holds no result of its final gate at all. The only exception is the red runs on earlier heads, and those are not the final tree. Step 4 of the next cycle takes "the list of the tests failing before the change from the final gate of the cycle of the merged branch ... from the merged branch's own copy of its cycle record". The change added "the CI run's result of step 12 included, with each check that failed named". That sentence says the copy holds something that step 12 of the same file says it does not hold. Step 4's own fallback then applies: "or the merged branch holds no copy of a cycle record with the result of its final gate, run the checks on the base". Two more gaps follow:
  - the CI row holds "each check by name and its conclusion", which means the jobs `tests` and `tests-windows`, not the names of failing tests;
  - `request_template.md:485-487` ("the list of failing tests it took from the earlier cycle's final gate") inherits the same gap.
- Why it matters: in the usual case the base of a cycle is the merge of the previous cycle's branch. Step 4 skips the base run in that case so that "a full run of the gate" is not added "to the wait of every cycle" (`SKILL.md:159-160`). After this change, every cycle that follows a cycle whose final gate was CI runs a full local gate on its base. The local full run is not removed, only moved from the end of one cycle to the start of the next. That defeats the task's *Need*: "testy biegną dwa razy, a komputer jest zajęty przez czas lokalnego przebiegu". It also breaks the premise of the answer to "Które przebiegi pełnej bramki przechodzą do CI?", which says the base run stays local because "oba zdarzają się rzadko". An orchestrator that reads only the changed sentence, and not the fallback, would instead look for a CI result in a copy that has none.
- Evidence: `SKILL.md:166-167` ("holds the final gate's result in its *Verification*, the CI run's result of step 12 included") against `SKILL.md:619-621` and `record_template.md:157-159` ("The committed copy holds the row of the CI run only for the runs before its own head"). The orchestrator knows at step 10 that CI will be the final gate, before the copy is made, but nothing writes that into the copy, and nothing gives step 4 another source for the result (for example the merged request's *Checks*, which step 12 edits after the run, or the run on the merged head read through the host's tool).
- Within the task: To do 1, the base run of step 4 staying local and rare, and the *Need*.

### I-2. The statement "the final gate ran in CI" never reaches the record the audit reads, and the time of a fallback local run never reaches it either (To do 5, row 6)

- Place: `task-cycle/skill/SKILL.md:492-494` and `:613-621`, `task-cycle/record_template.md:149-159`; read by `process-audit/audit_count.py` (`read_optional`, line 1603; `gate_rows`, line 2765) and `process-audit/SKILL.md:200-205`; commits `b646084` and `b2f3f82`.
- What is wrong: To do 5 asks: "*Verification* zapisu cyklu mówi wprost, że końcowa bramka biegła w CI, a audyt procesu ... podaje jako możliwą przyczynę ... także końcową bramkę w CI, odsyłając do *Verification* zapisu". The audit reads the committed copies under `docs/task-cycles/` (`audit_count.py:47`, `RECORDS = "docs/task-cycles/"` at line 345). Its new message ends "(the record's *Verification* says which)". Step 10 says the statement goes into the record's *Verification*. Step 12 writes it together with the CI row ("into *Verification* of the original record, with the statement that the final gate ran in CI"). `record_template.md` puts it inside that row ("the row is the run on its last head ... with the statement that the final gate ran in CI and that the cycle has no local time of it"), and that row is left out of the copy. The *Verification* of the copy the audit points to therefore never says that the final gate ran in CI. A cycle with its final gate in CI and a cycle whose `gate_times.py` failed look the same there, which is exactly what row 6 was to prevent ("czy ... zostawić fałszywe „pomiar się zepsuł”"). The same ordering loses the time of the fallback run. Row 6 and the answer to "Co audyt procesu robi z czasami bramki…" keep the measuring for "przebiegów lokalnych (awaryjnych, ...)". The 30-minute fallback run of step 12 is made on the head pushed after the copy's pair, with no repeat of the pair. So its time is appended to the ignored `gate_times.md` and never enters a copy the audit reads.
- Why it matters: the audit's pointer leads to a record that does not answer it, and the local times that the task keeps measured are lost for the fallback runs.
- Evidence: the lines above. The decision that CI is the final gate is taken at step 10, before "The record before it is committed", so the statement could be written into *Verification* before the copy is made. Only the CI result itself comes later.
- Within the task: To do 5, row 6.

### I-3. WAY-160 no longer says that the gate runs on the cycle's own system for a request left blocked for unfinished code, which the cycle's files do

- Place: `foundations/decisions.md:2809-2828` (WAY-160, both binding paragraphs, and *Verification* at 2843-2845) against `task-cycle/skill/blocked_finding.md:8-9, 21-22`, `task-cycle/skill/SKILL.md:433-434` (9.4) and `:483-485`; commit `b646084`.
- What is wrong: the old first sentence ("The task-cycle orchestrator itself runs the product's gate on the tree that goes into the request") covered every request. The new entry names the cycle's own run only for these cases: the base, the pin move, "where CI runs on no request", and the 30-minute fallback. Its first case of CI applies only to "a request whose code is finished". A request blocked for unfinished code on a product with CI falls under neither, and the entry says only that such a request "is out of that wait, its description saying that the result of the CI run stands on the request's page and was not waited for". Its *Verification* (complies) says the same and nothing about a local run. The cycle's files run the gate locally for that request: `blocked_finding.md` says "Run the gate on the change on your own system as step 10 gives its local run ... and the gate that ran on your own system stands for the final gate", and step 10 lists "for a request left blocked for unfinished code" among the local runs. The task's *Context* states it too: "Prośba zablokowana na niedokończonym kodzie nie czeka na CI, więc bramka biegnie dla niej lokalnie jak dziś."
- Why it matters: WAY-263 counts a foundation entry the change writes as Important when it restates the rule differently or without a qualification. A reader of WAY-160 alone finds no final gate for a blocked request on a product with CI. A later change that follows the entry could drop the local run that the files make, and the human would then get a blocked request with no gate.
- Evidence: the quoted lines. The fix is one clause in the first paragraph and its *Verification*, for example "...and for a request the cycle leaves blocked for unfinished code".
- Within the task: To do 1 (the sentence of WAY-160), rows 1 and 2.

### I-4. The change adds to `process-audit/audit_count.py`, a file over the threshold, without first moving a part of it out (WAY-365)

- Place: `process-audit/audit_count.py:459-465` (new `ABSENCE_CAUSES`), `:1598-1604`, `:2765`, module description `:227-228`; commit `b2f3f82`, the first commit of the change.

The file stood at 3310 lines on the base and stands at 3316 on the head, against the threshold of 1000 (WAY-385). No move commit exists. The method does not let a rationale in the author's report lower the grade. WAY-365's *Verification* (violates) names "an author who adds in place", and WAY-363 grades a finding about a file over a threshold "as any other finding".
- Why it matters: the rationale of WAY-365 is that an addition to a file not yet cleaned "stops neither the test nor the review" while the invariant `function-and-file-size` stands marked violated. Without the move, the file grows with no counterweight, which is the case the rule exists for.

- **M-3** (within the task). `foundations/decisions.md:2691-2694`, WAY-16 *Rejected*. The two added items are written for the-way and not for a product: "an error of the installer with junctions on Windows shows only in use" and "on the machine of the work, only the directories with Windows tests". Writing them as reasons in general terms ("the tests that need the other system run nowhere"), with the-way's case as an example, would fit the entry better. I found no sentence a reader would act on wrongly, so the finding stays Minor.
