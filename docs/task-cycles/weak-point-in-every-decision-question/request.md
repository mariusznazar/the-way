## What you will see

From now on, every time an agent of the process puts a choice to you, it gives you one more thing: the strongest argument against the way it recommends. The way open now also carries what undoing it later would cost, in one word where the cost is nothing. Where the recommended way is a shortcut and another way would be right if cost did not matter, the argument names that way and says why the agent does not recommend it. When you take the recommended way as it was offered, and the argument was based on the material, you are not asked a second question after your choice; in every other case the check question after your choice stays.

The same two things appear in the items of a request description where you choose: the part with each way's cost now says what undoing it costs, and the recommendation comes with its weak point in one sentence. Both examples in the description template show it.

Two more places change. In the rounds that define a project, an agent may now ask a follow-up question after your answer — either "you wrote this; from it follows that; does that hold?", or the reason behind a decision that is costly to reverse when neither your words nor the material give it. Refining a task keeps its old rule. And the weekly audit of the process no longer asks only "weigh it further or set it aside": where the rows show something broken that a step can fix, it puts that to you as a decision with a recommendation based on what is broken, never on how often the matter came back, and the note of the audit gets a third outcome, "decided".
